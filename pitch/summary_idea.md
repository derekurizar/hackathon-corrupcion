# The Idea — One-Page Summary

> Working title (configurable): **Expediente Público** / **Verdad sin Filtro** (ES) ·
> **Open Contract Newsroom** (EN)

---

## The one-liner

**We turn a year of Guatemala's public spending into investigative stories
anyone can read, hear, and trust — in 60 seconds.**

---

## The problem (in plain words)

Every contract the Guatemalan government signs is *already public*. The data
exists. It sits on an open portal called Guatecompras, in a raw technical
format (OCDS).

But "public" is not the same as "understandable."

- A citizen can't read it.
- A journalist needs days to find one lead inside millions of rows.
- Even officials don't have the time or tools to connect the dots.

So corruption doesn't hide in the dark. **It hides in plain sight — buried
under data nobody can read.** Transparency without comprehension is not
transparency.

And the scale is staggering: in this dataset, roughly **94.5% of all public
procurement is "direct purchase"** — money awarded without open competition.
That single number should be on every front page. Today it's invisible.

---

## The insight

The bottleneck is not *access* to data. It's **translation** — from raw
records into a story a human can act on, with the evidence attached.

---

## The solution

A three-step engine that runs automatically:

1. **Detect.** A deterministic rule engine scans a full year of contracts and
   flags unusual patterns — one supplier capturing most of an institution's
   money, suspiciously low competition, repeated direct awards. Rules, not
   guesswork: every flag is reproducible.

2. **Narrate.** Claude turns each flagged pattern into a clear,
   newsroom-quality story — **in Spanish and English** — but only from the
   structured evidence. It explains; it never invents.

3. **Present.** Each story becomes a cinematic, interactive "investigation
   article" with motion, charts, and a relationship map — plus a **60-second
   podcast** narrated in native Spanish and English. Read it, scroll it,
   present it, or just listen.

---

## The ethical guardrail (non-negotiable, and a feature)

The system **never says "this is corruption."** It surfaces *contracts and
patterns worth reviewing*. Real people who are individual suppliers are
**anonymized**. Every story and every podcast carries a mandatory caveat. If
the AI ever drifts, it falls back to a plain evidence summary automatically.

This restraint is not a limitation — it's what makes a journalist, an auditor,
or an institution able to *use* the output without legal risk.

---

## Who it's for

- **Journalists (primary)** — find defensible leads in minutes, with the
  evidence trail already built.
- **Citizens** — finally understand where the money goes, via plain language
  and the 60-second podcast.
- **Auditors & civil society** — prioritize which contracts to review first.

---

## Why now / why it scales

Guatecompras follows **OCDS — the global Open Contracting Data Standard**, used
by dozens of countries. We built for Guatemala first, but the engine is
data-standard-based, not country-specific. The same pipeline can light up
public spending **anywhere OCDS exists.** Guatemala is the proof; the world is
the market.

It runs fully automated and pre-generated, so it's cheap to operate and always
fast for the reader.
