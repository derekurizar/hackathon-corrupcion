# Pitch Deck (EN) — "One Case Through the Machine"

**Audience:** non-technical (judges, citizens, journalists, funders).
**Total time:** ~6–8 minutes + demo. **Tone:** sober, confident, documentary —
never sensational. Let the data and the product do the drama.

**The spine:** open on a number, then follow **one real contract** end to end
through the machine — ingest → detect → benchmark → narrate → present. The demo
is that same contract, alive. Close by calling back to the opening number.

How to read this file. Each slide has:
- **① On screen** — what the audience sees. A number, *one* line, or *one*
  image. Never a paragraph. The slide is not the script.
- **② Say this** — your spoken track. Speak it, don't read it.
- **③ Notes** — timing, delivery, why the slide exists.

**Brand:** *Open Contract Newsroom* (EN) / *Expediente Público* (ES).

> **The Machine Strip:** a 5-dot tracker shown along the bottom of slides 5–9:
> `INGEST · DETECT · BENCHMARK · NARRATE · PRESENT`. It lights up one dot per
> slide so the room always sees where "our one contract" is in the journey.

## Slide 12 — Signals, Not Proof

**① On screen**
Big:
> **"Signals, not proof."**

**② Say this**
"We made a hard rule and built it into the code: the system never accuses. It
surfaces contracts and patterns *worth reviewing*. Private individuals are
anonymized. Every story and podcast carries the caveat. If the AI ever drifts,
it falls back automatically to a plain evidence summary. This restraint isn't a
weakness — it's exactly what lets a journalist, an auditor, or an institution
actually *use* this."

**③ Notes**
~25s. Counter-intuitive but powerful: your *limits* are a selling point.
Funders and judges trust restraint. Say it with conviction, not apology.
Protect this slide if time is cut.

## Appendix — Q&A prep

- **"Isn't this just ChatGPT for contracts?"**
  No. The data and twenty-three fixed rules produce the evidence; the AI is
  only allowed to narrate what's already proven, and we validate it
  automatically. A traceable assistant, not a text generator. (Slides 6–8, 11.)

- **"Are you accusing companies of corruption?"**
  No, by design and in code. We surface "review signals," never proof.
  Individuals are anonymized; every output carries the caveat. (Slide 12.)

- **"How accurate is the detection?"**
  Deterministic — rule-based and reproducible, not a black box. The same data
  always yields the same flags.

- **"Why bilingual?"**
  The audience is Guatemalan citizens and journalists (Spanish) and the
  international transparency / funder community (English). UI and story are
  fully ES/EN; the story is generated in both in a single pass.

- **"What's the business / sustainability model?"**
  Built on a global standard (OCDS), automated and cheap to run. Expansion:
  other OCDS countries; users: newsrooms, anti-corruption NGOs, audit
  institutions, transparency funders.

- **"What's next?"**
  Broader rule coverage, more visual scene types, more months and countries
  ingested — the engine extends without redesign.
