# Pitch Deck — Slide-by-Slide Guide

**Audience:** non-technical (judges, citizens, journalists, funders).
**Total time:** ~6–8 minutes + demo. **Tone:** sober, confident, documentary —
never sensational. Let the data and the product do the drama.

How to read this file: each slide has **① On screen** (what the audience sees),
**② Say this** (your talk track — speak it, don't read it), and **③ Notes**
(delivery, timing, why this slide exists).

> *Expediente Público* / *Verdad sin Filtro* / *Open Contract Newsroom*.

## Slide 1 — The Hook

**① On screen**
Near-black screen. One huge line in condensed type:
> **"Every government contract in Guatemala is already public."**
Beat. Then below, smaller:
> *"So why can't anyone read them?"*
[BRAND] logo, small, bottom corner.

**② Say this**
"Everything the government spends is, by law, published online. The data is
already there. And yet — almost nobody can actually read it. That gap is the
whole story tonight."

**③ Notes**
~15 seconds. Don't rush. The silence after the first line is the pitch. No
agenda slide, no "about us" — open on tension.

## Slide 2 — The Problem

**① On screen**
Left: a screenshot of raw OCDS / Guatecompras data — walls of JSON / technical
records. Right, three short lines fading in:
- A citizen can't read this.
- A journalist needs days to find one lead.
- An auditor can't keep up with the volume.

**② Say this**
"This is what 'transparency' actually looks like today: a raw technical feed

Transparency without comprehension isn't transparency."

**③ Notes**
~30s. The screenshot must look genuinely overwhelming — that *is* the
argument. Land the line: "Transparency without comprehension isn't
transparency."

## Slide 4 — Why Existing Solutions Don't Solve It

**① On screen**
Two failing paths:
- **Open-data portals** → "Accurate. Unreadable."
- **Generic AI summaries** → "Readable. Untrustworthy — it can invent."

Arrow pointing down to a gap labeled **"the missing layer: explainable
storytelling."**

**② Say this**
"Two things exist today. Open-data portals — accurate but unreadable. And
generic AI — readable, but it can hallucinate, and you can't put an
unverifiable accusation in front of a journalist or a court. Both fail the
people who need this most. The missing layer is *explainable storytelling*:
readable like a newspaper, traceable like an audit."

**③ Notes**
~25s. This slide pre-empts the obvious "isn't this just ChatGPT?" question.
Kill it now, not in Q&A.

## Slide 5 — The Solution (the core)

**① On screen**
Three big steps, left to right, with simple icons:
**1. DETECT** → **2. NARRATE** → **3. PRESENT**
- DETECT: "Rules scan a year of contracts, flag unusual patterns."
- NARRATE: "AI writes the story — only from the evidence. Bilingual."
- PRESENT: "Cinematic article + 60-second podcast."

**② Say this**
"Our engine does three things, automatically. **One — Detect.** Fixed,
reproducible rules scan a full year of contracts and flag unusual patterns:
one company capturing most of an institution's money, suspiciously low
competition, repeated direct awards. **Two — Narrate.** Claude turns each flag
into a newsroom-quality story, in Spanish and English — but only from the
structured evidence. It explains; it never invents. **Three — Present.** Each
story becomes a cinematic, interactive article and a sixty-second podcast you
can just listen to."

**③ Notes**
~40s. This is the spine of the pitch. Slow down on the three verbs. "It
explains; it never invents" is a keeper line.

rehearse it until it's smooth.

**① On screen**
Split panel: a sentence from a story on the left; on the right, the exact

Header: **"Every claim is traceable. That's the moat."**

**② Say this**
"This is why we're not 'an AI that writes about contracts.' We're a

**③ Notes**
~25s. For a non-technical audience, "the data drives it, the AI just narrates,

## Slide 8 — The Ethical Guardrail

**① On screen**
Big: **"Signals, not proof."**
Three icons + lines:
- Never says "this is corruption" — surfaces "contracts worth reviewing."
- Real individuals anonymized.
- Mandatory caveat in every story and every podcast; safe automatic fallback.

**② Say this**
"We made a hard rule and built it into the code: the system never accuses. It
surfaces contracts and patterns *worth reviewing*. Private individuals are
anonymized. Every story and podcast carries the caveat. If the AI ever drifts,
it automatically falls back to a plain evidence summary. This restraint isn't a
weakness — it's exactly what lets a journalist, an auditor, or an institution
actually *use* this."

**③ Notes**
~30s. Counter-intuitive but powerful: your *limits* are a selling point.
Funders and judges trust restraint. Say it with conviction, not apology.

## Appendix — Q&A prep (anticipated questions)

- **"Isn't this just ChatGPT for contracts?"**
  No. The data and fixed rules produce the evidence; the AI is only allowed to
  narrate what's already proven, and we validate it automatically. It's a
  traceable assistant, not a text generator. (See Slide 7.)

- **"Are you accusing companies of corruption?"**
  No, by design and in code. We surface "review signals," never proof.
  Individuals are anonymized; every output carries the caveat. (Slide 8.)

- **"How accurate is the detection?"**
  Detection is deterministic — rule-based and reproducible, not a black box.
  The same data always yields the same flags.

- **"Why bilingual?"**
  The audience is Guatemalan citizens and journalists (Spanish) and the
  international transparency/funder community (English). UI, story, and audio
  are all fully ES/EN — including separate native podcast voices.

- **"What's the business / sustainability model?"**
  Built on a global standard (OCDS), automated and cheap to run. Expansion
  path: other OCDS countries; users: newsrooms, anti-corruption NGOs,
  audit institutions, transparency funders.

- **"What's next?"**
  Broader rule coverage, more visual scene types, more months/countries
  ingested — the engine is built to extend without redesign.

## Delivery cheat-sheet

- **Total:** ~6–8 min talk + demo. If cut short, protect Slides 3, 5, 6, 8.
- **Demo is the pitch.** Pre-load it. Have a recorded fallback. Never debug
  live.
- **Tone:** documentary, not activist. The restraint *is* the credibility.
- **Three lines to nail:** "Transparency without comprehension isn't
  transparency." · "It explains; it never invents." · "Signals, not proof."
- **Open and close on the same idea** (Slides 1 ↔ 12). Always.
